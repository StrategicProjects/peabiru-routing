Isochrones in the browser

Everything on this map is computed here, in your browser, by the peabiru npm package compiled to WebAssembly: the network is built from an OpenStreetMap extract and GTFS feeds, and every click routes from the point you chose. Nothing is sent to a server.

How this page works

The page loads the peabiru npm package as a plain ES module, fetches the .pbf and GTFS .zip files of the city, and calls buildNetwork() once. The street background is the street network itself, as returned by isochrone(…, { polygonOutput: false }), so no map tiles are needed. Each click calls isochrone() with the chosen mode and cutoffs and draws the GeoJSON polygons on a canvas. Public transport uses the median of the departures in the 10 minutes from the chosen time; the Porto Alegre bus feed only has trips from late morning to early evening, so pick a time between 12:00 and 17:00 there. Recife and Caruaru have streets only: no open GTFS feed was found for either, so public transport is not offered there; their extracts are Recife's municipality, as cut by IBGE's boundary, and Caruaru's urban area (14 × 12 km around the centre), without buildings (the Recife and Caruaru example explains how they are made). Elevation is not modelled, so walking and cycling on Caruaru's hills are faster here than in reality. The code is in site/demo, and the JavaScript guide walks through it.
