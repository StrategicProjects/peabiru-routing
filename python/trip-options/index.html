    Trip options¶

    trip_options() is peabiru's own trip planner: for each origin and destination it gives every trip worth considering, the ones that no other trip beats on all the chosen criteria at once. Where detailed_itineraries() answers "what is the fastest way there?", trip_options() answers "what are my choices?": faster but dearer, cheaper but slower, fewer transfers, less walking.

    Not dominated¶

    A trip dominates another when it is no worse on every chosen criterion and better on at least one. The criteria are

    • "TIME": door-to-door minutes from the departure minute, waits included;
    • "FARE": the fare by a fare structure (see Fares);
    • "TRANSFERS": rides minus one; a trip without public transport has 0, as many as a trip with a single ride;
    • "WALK": minutes on foot: the access and egress legs when they are walked, and the walks between stops (a bicycle or car leg counts 0).

    By default all four are used with a fare structure, all but "FARE" without one. At each departure minute of the window the options are the set of trips not dominated, with one trip per combination of values.

    The search is exact, to the second. What is returned is then compared at the resolution the values are read at: total time to a tenth of a minute, as it is reported, and walking to the whole minute (rounded half up; walk_resolution sets the step in seconds, and 1 compares the exact seconds). Of the trips of a minute, one is left out when another is no worse on every criterion in those terms and better on one, or equal on all of them and better in exact terms (less total time, then fewer transfers, less walking, a lower fare). Without this, a trip that is slower, dearer and with more transfers would be returned only because it walks a few seconds less. The values reported stay exact. Rounding cannot make a trip look better than one that beats it to the second, so the result is the same as comparing every trip at that resolution.

    The trips follow the rules of the other functions (the same access and egress searches, transfers between stops and boarding rules as detailed_itineraries()), so the times agree with the other functions.

    The examples use the sample data of Porto Alegre from the r5r project and its fare structure, downloaded once into the temporary directory, as in the Fares guide.

    import datetime as dt
import tempfile
import urllib.request
from pathlib import Path

import pandas as pd

import peabiru

poa = Path(tempfile.gettempdir()) / "peabiru-poa"
poa.mkdir(exist_ok=True)
url = "https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ipea/r5r/v2.5.0/r-package/inst/extdata/poa/"
for name in ["poa_osm.pbf", "poa_eptc.zip", "poa_trensurb.zip", "poa_hexgrid.csv"]:
    if not (poa / name).exists():
        urllib.request.urlretrieve(url + name, poa / name)
fares_zip = Path(tempfile.gettempdir()) / "peabiru-fares_poa.zip"
if not fares_zip.exists():
    urllib.request.urlretrieve(url + "fares/fares_poa.zip", fares_zip)

network = peabiru.build_network(poa)
fares = peabiru.read_fare_structure(fares_zip)
points = pd.read_csv(poa / "poa_hexgrid.csv")
origin = points[points["id"] == "89a90128053ffff"]
destination = points[points["id"] == "89a90129c9bffff"]
departure = dt.datetime(2019, 5, 13, 14, 0)

    Time and fare¶

    With time and fare as the criteria, the options of this pair over the ten minutes from 14:00 are a ride on the T4 bus, a faster trip with a transfer to the T1D (two fares, with the integration discount: R$ 7.20 instead of R$ 9.60), and walking, which is free:

    res = peabiru.trip_options(
    network, origin, destination, departure_datetime=departure,
    criteria=["TIME", "FARE"], fare_structure=fares,
)
cols = ["option", "total_time", "fare", "transfers", "walk_time", "departure_time",
        "n_departures", "description"]
print(res.options[cols].to_string(index=False))

    network, origin, destination, departure_datetime=departure, time_window=3,
    criteria=["TIME", "FARE"], fare_structure=fares, by_departure=True,

    All four criteria and the legs¶

    With the default criteria (all four, here, since there is a fare structure) the trade-offs with transfers and walking come in too. The legs of each option are in itineraries, in the format of detailed_itineraries() and keyed by from_id, to_id and option (a geopandas.GeoDataFrame, or a pandas.DataFrame with drop_geometry=True):

    res = peabiru.trip_options(
    network, origin, destination, departure_datetime=departure,
    fare_structure=fares, drop_geometry=True,
)
print(len(res.options), "options")
legs = res.itineraries
print(legs[legs["option"] == 1][["segment", "mode", "cumulative_fare", "segment_duration",
                                 "wait", "distance", "route"]].to_string(index=False))

    max_options keeps the first options of each pair, max_fare and max_trip_duration leave out the trips beyond them, and itineraries=False skips the legs.

    Fares are not additive¶

    A fare is not the sum of its rides: a transfer within the time allowance is discounted, some types have unlimited transfers, a cap may apply. Two partial trips are only compared on fare when one is at least as cheap for every way both can continue; the comparison makes no assumption about the fare structure (a discounted transfer may even cost more than two full fares). This exactness has a price: partial trips that could still get a discount are kept apart until they cannot, which makes the fare criterion the slowest one.

    Frequency-based routes¶

    GTFS frequencies.txt gives a headway, not departure times. The other functions draw random departures (Monte Carlo); trip_options() does not: a frequency-based vehicle is boarded at its expected time, half a headway after the traveller is ready to board (or after the period starts), within the period, and with exact_times = 1 at the first scheduled departure. The results do not change from call to call, and differ on purpose from those draws. The Sao Paulo sample shipped with the package runs by frequency:

    spo = peabiru.build_network(peabiru.sample_data_path())
pts = peabiru.sample_points()
res = peabiru.trip_options(
    spo, pts.iloc[[0]], pts.iloc[[26]], departure_datetime=departure,
    time_window=5, itineraries=False,
)
print(res.options[["option", "total_time", "transfers", "walk_time", "n_departures",
                   "description"]].to_string(index=False))

    Compared with the other functions¶

    • pareto_frontier() gives, for each fare cutoff, the percentiles of the travel time over Monte Carlo draws of departure times: a summary of time against budget, without the trips.
    • detailed_itineraries() gives the fastest trip, or with shortest_path=False the alternatives (one per sequence of routes, the fastest of each).
    • trip_options() gives the trade-offs themselves, trip by trip, with their legs, on any of four criteria. With criteria=["TIME"] and time_window=1, its first option has the total time of detailed_itineraries()'s fastest trip for that minute.
