    Scenarios¶

    The routing functions take three arguments that change the street network for one call, without building it again:

    • new_carspeeds: new car speeds, by OpenStreetMap way or inside polygons;
    • carspeed_scale: a factor for the car speeds of every other street;
    • new_lts: new levels of traffic stress (LTS) for cycling, by OpenStreetMap way or near lines.

    They are accepted by travel_time_matrix(), accessibility(), expanded_travel_time_matrix(), arrival_travel_time_matrix() and detailed_itineraries(). A scenario changes the street search from the origins (the trip without public transport, and access to it by bicycle or car) but not the egress from the stops. The examples use the sample network; the OpenStreetMap ids come from its primary roads:

    import geopandas as gpd
import pandas as pd

import peabiru

network = peabiru.build_network(peabiru.sample_data_path(), n_threads=2)
points = peabiru.sample_points()
edges = peabiru.street_network_to_sf(network)["edges"]
primary = edges[edges["street_class"] == "PRIMARY"]
primary_ids = primary["osm_id"].unique()


def summary(mode, **scenario):
    """Pairs reached and their mean travel time, by one street mode."""
    ttm = peabiru.travel_time_matrix(network, points, points, mode=mode,
                                     n_threads=2, **scenario)
    return len(ttm), round(float(ttm["travel_time_p50"].astype(float).mean()), 2)


print(len(primary_ids), "primary ways;", "by car:", summary("CAR"))

    88 primary ways; by car: (729, 7.46)

    Car speeds by way¶

    new_carspeeds as a DataFrame has columns osm_id, max_speed and speed_type. With speed_type="scale", max_speed multiplies the car speed of the way's edges; with "km/h" it replaces it. A value of 0 closes the way to cars. carspeed_scale multiplies the speeds of the edges new_carspeeds leaves out (all of them without new_carspeeds):

    slower = pd.DataFrame({"osm_id": primary_ids, "max_speed": 0.5, "speed_type": "scale"})
at_20 = pd.DataFrame({"osm_id": primary_ids, "max_speed": 20.0, "speed_type": "km/h"})
closed = pd.DataFrame({"osm_id": primary_ids, "max_speed": 0.0, "speed_type": "scale"})
print("primary roads at half speed:", summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=slower))
print("primary roads at 20 km/h:   ", summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=at_20))
print("primary roads closed:       ", summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=closed))
print("every street at 80%:        ", summary("CAR", carspeed_scale=0.8))
print("20 km/h, the rest at 80%:   ", summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=at_20, carspeed_scale=0.8))

    primary roads at half speed: (729, 7.54)
primary roads at 20 km/h:    (729, 7.6)
primary roads closed:        (651, 7.52)
every street at 80%:         (729, 8.14)
20 km/h, the rest at 80%:    (729, 8.26)

    With the primary roads closed, one of the points, which snaps to one of them, can only reach itself by car, and so 78 pairs are lost.

    Car speeds in polygons¶

    new_carspeeds as a GeoDataFrame of polygons in WGS 84 has columns poly_id (strings), scale (a factor) and priority (integers). Each street edge takes the factor of the polygon it meets with the highest priority (ties: the one it overlaps most); carspeed_scale applies to the edges outside every polygon:

    bbox = peabiru.street_network_bbox(network, "vector")
centre = gpd.points_from_xy([(bbox["xmin"] + bbox["xmax"]) / 2],
                            [(bbox["ymin"] + bbox["ymax"]) / 2])
zone = gpd.GeoDataFrame(
    {"poly_id": ["centre"], "scale": [0.3], "priority": [1]},
    geometry=centre.buffer(0.005),  # about 500 m around the centre
    crs="EPSG:4326",
)
print("30% of the speed within 500 m of the centre:", summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=zone))

    30% of the speed within 500 m of the centre: (729, 9.66)

    Levels of traffic stress¶

    max_lts (2 by default) is the highest level of traffic stress, from 1 to 4, a cyclist rides on. new_lts changes the level of streets: as a DataFrame with columns osm_id and lts (integers), or as a GeoDataFrame of lines in WGS 84 with columns line_id, lts (1 to 4) and priority, where each street edge takes the level of the line closest to it among those whose bounding box meets its own:

    calm = pd.DataFrame({"osm_id": primary_ids, "lts": 1})
lanes = gpd.GeoDataFrame(
    {"line_id": primary["edge_index"].astype(str), "lts": 1, "priority": 1},
    geometry=primary.geometry.values,
    crs="EPSG:4326",
)
print("by bicycle:                 ", summary("BICYCLE"))
print("primary roads at LTS 1:     ", summary("BICYCLE", new_lts=calm))
print("cycle lanes along them:     ", summary("BICYCLE", new_lts=lanes))

    by bicycle:                  (729, 11.82)
primary roads at LTS 1:      (729, 11.21)
cycle lanes along them:      (729, 10.87)

    The lines lower the stress of more streets than the way ids do: an edge takes the level of the closest line whose bounding box meets its own, so the streets next to a primary road, whose bounding boxes meet a line's, get level 1 too.

    What is an error¶

    Car speeds are kept as 16-bit integers of centimetres per second. A scenario that does not fit them is an error rather than a result:

    • a car speed that would pass that range or come to 0 or less (a negative factor or speed, a polygon scale of math.inf or 0, a carspeed_scale far above 1), and a carspeed_scale beyond the 32-bit float range;
    • a carspeed_scale of NaN;
    • a polygon that is not valid, and a tie with no winner: two polygons of the same priority and different scales that overlap a way by the same length (give one of them a higher priority).
    backwards = pd.DataFrame({"osm_id": primary_ids, "max_speed": -1.0, "speed_type": "scale"})
try:
    summary("CAR", new_carspeeds=backwards)
except ValueError as e:
    print(str(e)[:72], "...")

    the scenario leaves 140 street edges open to cars with a speed of 0 or l ...

    On a network with an elevation raster, a call with a scenario runs without the slope costs, with a warning (see Elevation).
